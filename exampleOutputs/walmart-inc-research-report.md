# Walmart Inc. — FY 2026 and Q1-Q2 FY 2027

## Executive Summary

Walmart retains its position as the world's largest retailer with fiscal year 2026 revenue of $713.16 billion, up 4.73% year-over-year. The company is undergoing significant transformation driven by accelerating e-commerce growth (24% globally), high-margin advertising expansion (46% growth in Walmart Connect), and strategic market share gains particularly among higher-income consumers. Net income increased 12.64% to $21.89 billion in FY2026. However, recent guidance has been cautious, with management citing macroeconomic uncertainty, fuel price headwinds ($175 million impact in Q1 FY2027), and tariff concerns. The company expects full-year FY2027 net sales growth of 3.5% to 4.5% with adjusted EPS of $2.75 to $2.85, below Wall Street consensus of $2.96. Despite near-term caution, analyst consensus remains strongly bullish with a mean 12-month price target of $137.93, representing approximately 20% upside from current levels around $115.

## Financial Performance

Fiscal Year 2026 delivered solid results with revenue of $713.2 billion, up 4.7% (5.1% in constant currency) and operating income growth of 5.4% adjusted, outpacing sales growth. Net earnings reached $21.89 billion, an increase of 12.64%. Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended April 30, 2026) showed strong momentum with revenues of $177.8 billion, up 7.3%, and net income of $5.3 billion, up 18.8%. Adjusted EPS hit $0.66, meeting analyst expectations. Q2 FY2027 revenue reached $177.4 billion, up 4.8%, with net income surging 56% to $7.03 billion and EPS of $0.88 surpassing estimates by 21%. For full-year FY2027, management expects net sales growth of 3.5% to 4.5% and adjusted EPS of $2.75 to $2.85, below Wall Street consensus of $2.96 per share.

## E-Commerce & Digital Transformation

E-commerce is a transformational engine for Walmart. Q1 FY2027 saw e-commerce growth of 26%, while the advertising business surged 37%. FY2026 marked the eighth consecutive quarter of e-commerce growth above 20%, with the segment delivering 24% global growth and $150.4 billion in total e-commerce sales, now representing approximately 20% of company revenue. Walmart Connect advertising grew 41% (excluding Vizio), with local advertising business growing 46% to nearly $6.4 billion. Marketplace sales climbed nearly 50%, and expedited store-fulfilled delivery channels grew more than 50%, demonstrating the strength of omnichannel capabilities. This high-margin digital business is growing significantly faster than traditional retail operations.

## Market Share Gains & Customer Expansion

Walmart is capturing market share across all income demographics, with outsized gains among higher-income households. In Q1 FY2027, the fashion category achieved mid-single-digit growth with almost all increases coming from households with annual income over $100,000. CFO John David Rainey stated that speedy deliveries from stores are helping Walmart attract more shoppers, particularly those with higher incomes, and that the company's ability to serve customers at scale combined with delivery speed is translating into continued market share gains. Q2 FY2027 saw the strongest transaction growth in six quarters and best share growth in fashion in five years, even as consumer pressures persist. This demographic expansion is strategically significant as it reduces historical reliance on lower-income customer bases.

## Competitive Position & Scale

Walmart retains its position as the world's largest retailer, followed by Amazon and Germany's Schwarz Group, according to the 2026 National Retail Federation rankings. The company operates more than 10,900 stores and numerous e-commerce websites in 19 countries, serving approximately 280 million customers and members weekly with approximately 2.1 million associates worldwide. Walmart holds the strongest grocery position in the United States with food driving 59% of U.S. sales. The company's e-commerce growth of 26% outpaces Amazon's retail segment growth, though Amazon maintains advantages through infrastructure depth, AWS-funded margin flexibility, and 201 million Prime members. Walmart's market capitalization reached $1 trillion briefly in early 2026 before settling around $906.9 billion in mid-August 2026, reflecting its emergence as a technology-enabled retail powerhouse.

## Strategic Initiatives & Leadership

John Furner assumed the role of President and CEO on February 1, 2026, replacing Doug McMillon. Furner articulated a vision emphasizing the accelerating pace of change in retail and technology integration, stating that the company is moving more quickly to realize benefits of its business model. Key strategic initiatives include: (1) AI integration with generative AI now embedded in Walmart's search functionality; (2) Supply chain resilience through nearshoring, reducing Chinese manufacturing reliance from 80% to 60% in favor of India and Mexico sourcing; (3) Premium private label expansion, with Bettergoods launching in April 2024 and hitting $1 billion in run-rate sales faster than any prior private label launch in company history; and (4) Disciplined capital deployment with ROI lens applied to AI, automation, and store expansion investments to scale newer tech-powered businesses alongside core retail at lower marginal cost.

## Challenges & Macroeconomic Headwinds

Management faces significant near-term challenges. CFO John David Rainey described the consumer backdrop as 'somewhat unstable,' contributing to conservative full-year FY2027 guidance that triggered a 7% stock decline in February 2026. Fuel price headwinds represent a material pressure, with Q1 FY2027 absorbing a $175 million negative impact, expected to grow larger in Q2 if prices remain elevated. About one-third of Walmart's U.S. general merchandise is imported, with China remaining a meaningful share, making tariff escalation a direct margin event. Management explicitly excluded tariff assumptions from FY2027 guidance—an unusually prominent disclaimer signaling uncertainty. Structural operating margins of approximately 4.2% constrain strategic flexibility. Additionally, Amazon's full-spectrum competition, CEO transition execution risk during macro volatility, and potential mix shift toward lower-margin grocery pose ongoing threats. Consumer spending pressures are evident, though Walmart is successfully capturing share from higher-income shoppers trading down for value.

## Stock Valuation & Analyst Outlook

Walmart stock closed August 14, 2026 at $115.27, within its 52-week range of $95.42 to $135.16, below the all-time high of $134.20 reached on May 19, 2026. The stock trades at a P/E multiple of 40.15x trailing twelve months and 38.29x forward, commanding a premium to historical averages. According to 43 analysts, the average consensus rating is 'Buy,' with a mean 12-month price target of $137.93, implying approximately 20% upside. This includes 29 'Strong Buy' ratings, six 'Moderate Buys,' and four 'Holds.' Guggenheim recently lowered its price target to $135 from $137 while maintaining a Buy rating. The bull case rests on durable comparable sales momentum, 15.1% return on investment, scaling advertising business, and defensive sector positioning during consumer uncertainty. The valuation represents a premium some analysts characterize as 'priced for perfection,' reflecting expectations that Walmart's digital transformation and market share gains will drive sustained outperformance.
