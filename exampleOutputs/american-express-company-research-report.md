# American Express Company — Q2 2026 / FY 2025-2026

## Executive Summary

American Express is preparing to celebrate its 175th anniversary in March 2026, having evolved into a leading integrated payments company. The company has demonstrated strong financial momentum through 2024-2026, with full-year 2025 revenue reaching $67.0 billion, up 10% from fiscal 2024. Most recently, American Express reported second-quarter 2026 earnings that beat analyst expectations and raised its full-year revenue growth guidance to 10%, citing stronger-than-expected performance in the first half of 2026. Q2 2026 earnings per share reached $4.53, an 11% increase compared with $4.08 in the same period last year, with total revenues net of interest expense climbing 10% to $19.6 billion.

## Financial Performance and Results

For full year 2024, American Express reported net income of $10.1 billion, or $14.01 per share, representing 25% earnings per share growth. Momentum has accelerated into 2025-2026: FY 2025 revenues reached $67.0 billion (up 10% from FY 2024), with net income of $10.7 billion (up 7.1%). Q2 2026 earnings per share reached $4.53, an 11% increase compared with $4.08 in the same period last year. Total revenues net of interest expense climbed 10% to $19.6 billion in Q2 2026. The company's profit margin remained stable at approximately 17% through recent quarters.

## Forward Guidance and Outlook

For fiscal 2026, the company has set earnings-per-share guidance to a range of $17.30 to $17.90, and narrowed its full-year revenue outlook to $79.45 billion, representing about 10% year-over-year growth. Earlier 2025 guidance expected revenue growth of between 8 to 10 percent and EPS in the range of $15.00 to $15.50, with plans to increase the quarterly common stock dividend by 17 percent. The company has reaffirmed its full-year guidance multiple times in 2025 based on steady spend and credit trends, subject to macroeconomic conditions.

## Operating Metrics and Credit Performance

Billed business—the company's measure of overall card volume—increased 9% to $455.8 billion in Q2 2026. Card Member spending reached quarterly highs with record spending levels, and customer retention remained strong across the customer base. Consolidated provisions for credit losses totaled $1.1 billion in Q2 2026, compared with $1.4 billion in the year-earlier period, reflecting a reserve release during the quarter compared to a reserve build in the prior year. The net write-off rate held at 2.0%, flat year-over-year, demonstrating best-in-class credit performance and disciplined expense management.

## Business Segment Composition

American Express operates through four primary revenue segments. Global Consumer Services Group generated 48.02% of total revenue, Global Commercial Services 23.35%, International Card Services 17.93%, and Global Merchant and Network Services 10.70% in fiscal year 2025. The US Consumer Services segment is the undeniable engine of the company, generating $34.81 billion in revenue, representing slightly more than half of total net revenue, providing high-end consumer cards such as the iconic Gold and Platinum cards alongside extensive travel and lifestyle benefits. Global Commercial Services generated approximately $16.93 billion, International Card Services contributed meaningful growth, and merchant and network services rounded out the portfolio.

## Geographic Distribution and International Expansion

American Express's revenue is heavily concentrated in the United States, which accounts for 77.55% of total revenue. International expansion is underway with EMEA Region contributing 9.79%, JAPA Region 7.22%, and LACC Region 5.81% of revenue. While the U.S. remains the primary profit engine, American Express has expanded in the UK, Japan and Australia, leveraging premium positioning to attract affluent international consumers. The competitive position in international markets is notably weaker than its domestic fortress, but the moat still relies heavily on the aspirational brand value of the network.

## Competitive Positioning and Market Share

American Express operates in a highly competitive environment, facing direct competition from global payment networks like Visa and Mastercard, major credit card issuers such as Capital One and Synchrony, and diversified banks including Bank of America, Citigroup and JPMorgan Chase. The company's unique closed-loop system, connecting cardholders and merchants directly, sets it apart from open-loop networks. In 2024, Visa held a dominant global market share of 50.1%, followed by Mastercard with 31.8%, while American Express, operating a closed-loop model, captured 7.2% of the global market. As of early 2025, American Express is the third-largest global card network by purchase volume, behind Visa and Mastercard, with total network volume above $1.6 trillion annually, and controls about 19 percent of U.S. credit card purchase volume as the largest non-bank credit card issuer in the United States.

## Competitive Advantages and Differentiation

Key advantages underpinning American Express's market position include affluent customer base, strong brand, and differentiated merchant relationships that allow higher discount rates. Deep airline and hotel partnerships create captive ecosystems and acquisition funnels, while co-brand tie-ins with major carriers and hotel groups drive stable transaction flows and customer retention. Premium benefits including lounges and Fine Hotels & Resorts reinforce differentiation and reduce churn. The prime-heavy portfolio mix and closed-loop analytics enable superior risk-adjusted returns on equity, which frequently exceed 30 percent, with Common Equity Tier 1 ratio near 10.5 percent, above many peers in the banking sector.

## Growth Drivers and Strategic Priorities

Card Member spending, customer retention, demand for premium products, and credit performance continued to be strong across the customer base through 2024-2025. Gen Z and Millennials comprised over 60 percent of new consumer account acquisitions in 2024–2025, indicating successful targeting of younger affluent demographics. Management is confident in sustaining strong momentum over the long term, driven by attractive opportunities across the premium customer base, particularly with Millennial and Gen Z consumers and in key international markets, along with operating expense leverage which enables continued high-level investment in brand, value propositions, coverage, marketing, technology and talent. The company plans to continue investing at high levels to drive sustainable growth while controlling operating expenses and executing share repurchase programs.

## Analyst Outlook and Valuation

Analyst consensus indicates a mean recommendation of 'outperform' based on 30 analysts, with an average target price around $375.50 compared with recent closing prices near $340.81, implying roughly 10 percent upside potential. Current earnings expectations of approximately $17.67 per share for fiscal 2026, combined with a double-digit growth outlook and recurring quarterly beats, underpin higher price objectives from research firms. Recent research includes higher price targets from firms citing resilient spending trends, alongside more cautious updates from others focused on competition and valuation pressures. The stock is broadly in line with other large financial names pricing in mid-teens earnings growth against steady consumer and corporate spending.

## Risks, Challenges and Regulatory Headwinds

Merchant discount fee pressure globally compresses net take rates and could force pricing shifts. Potential U.S. regulatory actions on interchange or late fees threaten fee growth assumptions. Lounge crowding and rewards inflation raise costs to serve premium customers, requiring program economics tightening. Competition from potential industry combinations and wallet platforms intensifies market share battles. Macroeconomic conditions, changes in interest rates, effects of inflation, tariffs, and geopolitical instability present ongoing risks to consumer and business spending. The company's international competitive position remains notably weaker than its domestic fortress, though the brand moat relies heavily on aspirational network value.

## Capital Allocation and Shareholder Returns

The company planned to increase its quarterly common stock dividend by 17 percent as part of its 2025 capital allocation strategy. Shareholders receive quarterly dividends with recent distributions at $0.95 per share, resulting in a dividend yield of approximately 1.1%, which is lower than the industry average of 2.0%. The company maintains a disciplined share repurchase program and strong capital metrics that support growth and risk-taking in competitive markets. Returns on equity frequently exceed 30 percent, reflecting high-margin operations and efficient capital deployment.

## Conclusion and Investment Perspective

American Express has demonstrated sustained financial growth and operational excellence through 2024-2026, with record revenues, strong earnings growth, and consistent margin expansion. The company's premium closed-loop network, affluent customer base, and strategic focus on younger demographics (Millennials and Gen Z) position it well for continued expansion. Management's confident guidance for 8-10% revenue growth and 10% growth in full-year 2026, combined with best-in-class credit performance and disciplined expense management, underscore operational strength. However, regulatory headwinds on interchange and late fees, international competition, and merchant fee pressures remain challenges. With current analyst consensus favoring the stock and management guiding for continued double-digit growth trajectory, American Express appears well-positioned within the competitive payments landscape, though current valuation at elevated price targets reflects strong growth expectations already embedded in market pricing.
